During my summer vacation, I read Väsyneet Naiset by Hanne Kettunen. I picked it up from a shelf because of its beautiful cover, and I got sold on the soft matte touch. I didn't expect it to leave me thinking and discussing about invisible work for weeks afterward. 

 

### The Responsible Party (suomeksi: vastuunkantaja) 

The book sparked so many thoughts, which I can also recommend as discussion points for you and your friends, your mother, or your partner: 

Do you create the grocery list or pack the car for the summer road trip? Do you remind your partner five times to sign up for the unemployment fund and plan your mum’s birthday gift while your siblings are "hengessä mukana" (along for the ride)?  

These are the vastuunkantajat, the people who raise their hands to plan the team day at work or step in to help a stranger and their dog walk past angry geese by literally forming a human shield. Sound like anyone you know? Or perhaps you? 

 

### Metawork (suomeksi: metatyö) 

Kettunen introduces another great term for this kind of invisible labor: metawork. Let me give you an example. 

For Mother’s Day this year, I suggested to my brothers that we could go to the summer theater together, like we did as kids. They agreed (since they didn’t have other ideas), and we had a lovely day at Lurens Sommarteater in Loviisa. Everyone showed up, but there was a lot of metawork behind the scenes: coming up with the idea, checking show dates, booking tickets, figuring out the best seating, calling to confirm the "traditional" boiled long sausages were gluten-free, estimating how many sausages everyone would eat, and sending MobilePay requests for the tickets. 

This is metawork. It's the planning, remembering, anticipating, and following up that often goes unnoticed. I often find myself taking on this kind of work. Sometimes I even enjoy it; it gives me a sense of accomplishment, another task ticked off my to-do list. It’s the "I don’t mind doing it if no one else has time" kind of work. 

 

Discussion point: Ask your partner at a random moment, maybe when you’re trying to fall asleep, "What are you thinking about right now?" See what they’re thinking. Maybe they’re doing metawork too, or maybe they’re thinking about nothing… or just darkness.
